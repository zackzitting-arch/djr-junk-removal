Moving Out? What to Do With Everything You Are Not Taking

Published August 26, 2026 · DJR Junk Removal · Meridian, Boise & the Treasure Valley

Movers charge by weight or by volume. Which means every box you have not opened in three years is something you are about to pay, by the pound, to relocate.

Purge before you pack, not after

This is the whole thing. Sorting at the new house feels easier because it is future-you's problem, but it costs real money and it means unpacking into clutter. Every item you decide about before moving day is an item you do not pay to transport, carry twice, or find a place for.

A workable timeline

Four weeks out — the obvious stuff

Garage, basement, shed, the closet nobody opens. This is where the volume is and none of it is emotionally difficult. Broken things, outgrown things, the second set of everything.

Two weeks out — furniture decisions

Measure the new place. Furniture that does not fit is the single most common thing people move and then immediately get rid of. If the sectional will not work in the new living room, deal with it now, not on the truck.

Moving week — the last pass

Pantry, cleaning supplies, half-used paint, the garage shelf. Book the haul for the day after the movers leave, when you can finally see what is actually left.

Paint, chemicals and propane cannot go on the moving truck either. Most movers refuse them outright and so do we — they need household hazardous waste handling. Deal with those separately and early; discovering them at 6pm on moving day is a bad time.

What it usually runs

A typical post-move clear-out — leftover furniture, boxes, the garage remainder — lands somewhere between a quarter and a full load ($145–$495), depending mostly on whether the garage got dealt with.

Landlords and deposits

If you are renting, an empty unit is usually the difference between getting the deposit back and arguing about it. Most leases require the place emptied and broom-clean. Leaving a couch behind because it was too much hassle is a good way to have the cost of removing it deducted at a markup.
