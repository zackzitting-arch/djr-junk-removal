Shed, Deck and Playset Teardown: What Is Actually Involved

Published August 28, 2026 · DJR Junk Removal · Meridian, Boise & the Treasure Valley

Backyard structures are the one category where the hauling is the easy half. The work is getting the thing into pieces that can leave.

Check three things before anyone swings a hammer

  • Utilities. Sheds often have power run to them, sometimes buried and sometimes not to code. Water lines too. Find out what is feeding it before demolition starts.
  • Permits. Small accessory structures usually do not need one, but larger sheds and anything on a foundation sometimes do. Requirements differ between Boise, Meridian and unincorporated Ada County — a quick call to your city saves a real headache.
  • The material. Which decides where it goes, and what it costs.

Material matters more than size

Wood

The most common and the most straightforward. The one thing to know: pressure-treated lumber cannot be burned, ever, and older treated wood may need specific disposal handling. Do not let anyone tell you to have a bonfire with your old deck.

Metal

Metal sheds come apart fast and the material is recyclable. Sharp edges everywhere though — this is a gloves-and-boots job, not a flip-flops-on-a-Saturday job.

Plastic and resin

Playsets and resin sheds disassemble easily and are mostly low-risk. Bulky rather than heavy, so they take more truck than you would guess.

Older structures deserve a second look. Sheds and outbuildings built before the 1980s can contain asbestos in siding, roofing felt or transite panels. If the building is old and you are unsure what the siding is, stop and have it assessed. Asbestos is the one thing on this page where doing it yourself is genuinely dangerous.

What about the slab?

Concrete is heavy, priced differently from household junk, and often the thing people forget to plan for. If there is a pad under the shed and you want it gone too, say so up front — it changes the job.

What it costs

A small metal shed or a modest playset is usually a half load ($275). A large wood shed or a full deck teardown is typically a full load ($495) or more, and the demolition labor is priced with it. A photo of the structure and its surroundings is the fastest way to get a real number.
