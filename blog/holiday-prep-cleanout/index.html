Before the Holidays: Making Room for Guests Without a Weekend of Dump Runs

Published November 17, 2026 · DJR Junk Removal · Meridian, Boise & the Treasure Valley

The guest room has been a storage room since spring. The garage has no room for a second car and the weather has turned. And the calendar says people are coming. Here is how to do this in three evenings instead of three weekends.

Evening one: the guest room

Everything in it falls into one of three piles. Things that belong somewhere else in the house go there tonight. Things you are keeping but have no home for get one box, labeled, and go in the garage or a closet. Everything else — the broken chair, the mattress nobody should sleep on, the boxes from the last move you never opened — is the haul pile. Leave it where it is.

Evening two: the garage

Winter in the valley is the season you want the car inside. Walk the garage with the same three piles. Be ruthless with the things you kept "for a project": the project has had all year. Old patio furniture, the broken snowblower, the kids' outgrown bikes, the spare tires that fit a car you sold. Put the haul pile along one wall so it is easy to point at.

The spare freezer and fridge get a decision now. If the second fridge in the garage is going, unplug it, empty it and leave the door open for a day before pickup. It contains refrigerant, which carries its own fee on top of the load price, and it is the one item people forget to mention when they book.

Evening three: book it

Take a photo of each pile, send them over, and you have a range back usually within the hour. Book a slot on a weekday morning before the guests arrive and the whole thing leaves in one visit. We do the carrying; you do the pointing. Mon–Sat, 8am–6pm.

What it costs

A guest-room-and-garage tidy is commonly a quarter to half load ($290–$500). If the garage has been accumulating for years it can run to a three-quarter or full load ($630–$695). Volume is what sets the number, the crew confirms the price on site before loading, and stairs or appliances with refrigerant are the usual things that add to it — mention them when you send the photos.

After the holidays

Keep the system: the same three piles work for the post-Christmas round of outgrown toys, the old TV the new one replaced, and the tree stand that finally broke. See getting rid of old TVs and electronics for the one that takes a little more thought.

And every job we do between now and then puts $10 toward Make-A-Wish Idaho — details on our home page.
