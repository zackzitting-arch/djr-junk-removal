What Junk Removal Can and Cannot Take

Published August 20, 2026 · DJR Junk Removal · Meridian, Boise & the Treasure Valley

The honest answer is: almost everything. But the exceptions matter, and knowing them before the truck shows up saves everybody a wasted trip.

What we take, no questions

  • Furniture — couches, sectionals, mattresses and box springs, bed frames, dressers, desks, tables, that recliner nobody will admit to owning
  • Appliances — refrigerators, freezers, washers, dryers, dishwashers, ranges, water heaters
  • Electronics — TVs, monitors, computers, printers, stereo equipment
  • Yard and outdoor — patio furniture, grills, play sets, fencing, branches and yard debris
  • Construction debris — drywall, lumber, flooring, cabinets, fixtures from a remodel
  • The whole-property jobs — garage, basement, attic, shed, storage unit, estate and rental clear-outs
  • The awkward ones — hot tubs, pianos, safes, exercise equipment, above-ground pools

If it is not on this list, it is probably still fine. Ask.

What needs a heads-up

These are not problems — they just carry their own disposal handling, so tell us when you book rather than when we arrive:

  • Refrigerators, freezers and air conditioners. Anything with refrigerant has to be handled under specific rules. Perfectly routine, just not something to spring on a crew.
  • Mattresses and box springs. Widely accepted, usually with their own fee.
  • Tires. Most facilities cap how many they will take at once and charge per tire.
  • Televisions and monitors. Older sets in particular go through electronics recycling rather than general waste.
  • Pianos, safes and gun safes. Not a disposal issue, a weight and access issue. We need to know before we plan the job.

What no hauler can take

This list is not a policy choice — it is regulated, and any company that offers to take these off your hands is one you should think twice about.

  • Paint, stains, solvents and thinners
  • Motor oil, antifreeze, fuel and propane tanks
  • Pesticides, herbicides, pool chemicals and household chemicals
  • Asbestos — including older floor tile, pipe wrap and some popcorn ceilings
  • Medical and biohazard waste, including sharps
  • Ammunition, explosives and flares
  • Car batteries and most loose household batteries

Where these actually go. Most of the list above is handled through household hazardous waste collection. Check with Ada County for current drop-off locations, hours and any limits before you load up the car — the details change, and it is a short call that saves a wasted trip.

One important exception: if you suspect asbestos, do not disturb it, do not bag it, and do not transport it. That needs a licensed abatement contractor. It is the one item on this page where doing it yourself can genuinely hurt you.

A note on paint, because it comes up every time

Latex paint and oil-based paint are handled differently, and the rules are not intuitive. Rather than guess — or take advice from the internet about kitty litter — ask the county what they want you to do with the cans you actually have. Two minutes on the phone beats a rejected load.

When in doubt, send a photo

The fastest way to settle any of this is a picture. Text us a photo of the item or the pile and we will tell you straight away whether we can take it, what it will cost, and whether anything on it needs different handling. If we cannot take something, we will tell you who can.
