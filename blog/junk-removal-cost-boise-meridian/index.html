What Does Junk Removal Cost in Boise and Meridian?

Published August 20, 2026 · DJR Junk Removal · Meridian, Boise & the Treasure Valley

Almost every junk removal company in the Treasure Valley makes you call before they will tell you a price. We would rather just publish ours.

The short answer

Junk removal here is priced by volume — how much space your stuff takes up in the truck — not by weight and not by the hour. Our loads run from a $80 minimum up to $495 for a full truck.

Those prices include the labor. We carry everything out ourselves, and we sweep up after. You are not paying extra for the part where somebody actually lifts it.

Why volume, and not weight or hours

Weight-based pricing sounds fair until you try to use it. Nobody can tell what a garage weighs by looking at it, which means you get a vague estimate on the phone and a real number after the truck is already loaded. That is a bad place to find out you disagreed.

Hourly pricing has the opposite problem: it quietly rewards a slow crew.

Volume is the one measure both sides can see. You can look at a truck bed and understand what half of it means. That is why we built the load selector on our home page — click through quarter, half, three-quarter and full, and the bed fills up so you can match it against what is sitting in your garage.

What actually moves the number

How much there is

This is most of it. Everything else is a rounding error next to volume.

Where it is sitting

Curbside and garage jobs are the fastest. A third-floor walk-up, a tight basement stairwell, or a backyard with no gate access takes longer, and on a big job that can affect the quote. If access is awkward, mention it up front and we will factor it in before we arrive rather than after.

A few specific items

Some things carry their own disposal costs regardless of the space they take: mattresses, tires, appliances containing refrigerant, pianos, and hot tubs are the usual ones. None of these are a problem — they just are not priced purely by volume. Ask when you book.

What we cannot take at all

Hazardous material is the main category — paint, solvents, chemicals, asbestos. That is not a pricing question, it is a legal one. See what junk removal can and cannot take for the full picture.

A quick way to estimate your own job. Stand where the pile is and ask: would this fill a pickup bed? That is roughly a quarter load. Would it fill three pickup beds? That is roughly a full load. It gets most people within one tier, which is close enough to know what you are dealing with before you call.

How this compares to the alternatives

Renting a dumpster

A dumpster can genuinely be the better call — if your project runs over several days, if you are doing a renovation and generating debris as you go, and if you have driveway space you are willing to give up. The trade-offs people underestimate: you still load every item yourself, the container sits in your driveway the whole time, and some jurisdictions require a permit if it lands on the street.

If your junk already exists in a pile and you want it gone today, a dumpster is usually the more expensive way to do the same job, because you are also supplying the labor.

Doing it yourself

Renting a truck and making dump runs works, and for a small load it can be cheaper. Price the whole thing before you commit: truck rental, fuel, transfer station fees, and the fact that most household junk needs more than one trip. Then add your Saturday. For a lot of people the honest math lands closer than they expect — and that is before anybody throws out a back on a chest freezer.
