How to Get Ready for Junk Removal Day (So It Takes One Trip)

Published November 2, 2026 · DJR Junk Removal · Meridian, Boise & the Treasure Valley

You do not need to do much before we arrive. But the small amount you can do makes the visit faster, keeps the price where it was quoted, and means nothing you wanted to keep ends up on the truck.

The night before

  • Walk the pile and pull out anything you are keeping. Once it is in the truck it is sorted with everything else, so the moment to change your mind is before, not after.
  • Check pockets, drawers and boxes. Dressers and filing cabinets in particular. Keys, paperwork and the occasional envelope of cash turn up in furniture more often than anyone expects.
  • Pull the hazardous items. Paint, chemicals, fuel, propane tanks. We cannot take them, and finding them mid-load slows everything down. See what we can and cannot take.
  • Empty the fridge or freezer if one is going, and give it a day unplugged with the door propped open.

On the day

  • Clear the path. Move cars out of the driveway, unlock the side gate, and keep pets somewhere they will not be underfoot. A clear route is the single biggest thing that makes a job quick.
  • Point, do not carry. You do not need to stage anything at the curb. We do the lifting; that is what the price includes. Just show us what goes.
  • Mention the surprises. Stairs, a narrow basement door, an item heavier than it looks, anything with refrigerant. None of it is a problem, but all of it is easier to plan for from the driveway than discover halfway up a stairwell.

Stay or go, your call. Plenty of people hand us the garage code and go to work. If you would rather be there, be there. Either way we take before-and-after photos, sweep up, and confirm the price with you on site before anything gets loaded.

What you do not need to do

Bag it, box it, break it down, or drag it to the curb. If that makes you feel better, go ahead, but it does not change the price. Volume is what we charge for, and a disassembled bookshelf takes the same truck space as an assembled one lying on its side.

About the price

Our loads run from a $125 minimum to $695 for a full truck, and the number we gave you on the phone or from your photo is a range, not a surprise. The crew confirms it on site before loading. The only things that move it are the ones you tell us about first — extra volume, stairs, heavy material, or an appliance with refrigerant.
