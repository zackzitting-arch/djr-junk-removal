Taking Down an Old Fence: Posts, Panels and the Concrete Footings

Published November 12, 2026 · DJR Junk Removal · Meridian, Boise & the Treasure Valley

A leaning fence is the kind of project that looks like a Saturday and turns into three, because the part you can see — the panels — is the easy half. The posts are set in concrete, and the concrete is the half nobody budgets for.

Before you pull a single board

  • Talk to the neighbor. A shared fence is usually shared property. Even where it is clearly yours, the person on the other side will have an opinion about a week with no fence and a dog.
  • Call 811. Pulling posts means digging, and irrigation lines, gas and buried power run along property lines more often than you would think. The locate is free and takes a couple of days.
  • Check whether a permit applies. Replacing a fence usually does not need one in Boise or Meridian, but height, corner-lot sight lines and HOA rules can all come into play for the new one. Easier to know now.

What comes out, by material

Wood

Panels and rails come down fast with a drill and a pry bar. The posts are the issue: a 4x4 in a concrete footing either gets dug out whole, which is heavy, or cut off at grade and the stub left, which is quicker but means the new post goes somewhere else. Pressure-treated wood cannot be burned, so do not plan a bonfire.

Vinyl

Light, bulky, comes apart easily, and takes up a lot of truck for what it weighs. The posts are often sleeved over a wood or steel insert set in concrete.

Chain-link

Roll the fabric, unbolt the rails, and the metal is recyclable. The posts are set in concrete like everything else.

The footings are heavy material. A single post footing can weigh as much as a small appliance, and a long run of fence means dozens of them. Concrete is not priced like household junk — tell us up front whether the footings are coming out or staying in the ground, and the range you get will reflect it.

What it costs

If you have already taken the fence down and have a pile of panels and posts, that is a straightforward haul on our volume tiers — a modest run is commonly a quarter to half load ($290–$500), a long run of wood fencing a half to full. If you want the teardown done as well, say so; we price the demolition with the haul. Send a photo of the fence line and a rough length, and you get a range before we come.
