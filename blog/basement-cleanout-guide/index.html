Clearing a Basement Without Carrying Everything Up the Stairs Twice

Published November 4, 2026 · DJR Junk Removal · Meridian, Boise & the Treasure Valley

A full basement is the hardest cleanout in a house, and not because of what is in it. It is because every single item has to come up a flight of stairs, and most basement stairs were not designed with a chest freezer in mind.

Sort it where it sits

The mistake is carrying things up to decide about them. Decide downstairs. Work one corner at a time and make three piles: keep, give away, gone. Be quick about it — if something has been in a basement for five years without being missed, the answer is usually already made.

Keep moves upstairs or to a shelf. Give away gets photographed and listed. Gone stays exactly where it is until the truck arrives, because we are going to carry it anyway.

What basements usually hold

  • Old furniture that came down for storage and never left.
  • Appliances. A second fridge or a freezer is very common, and both contain refrigerant, which carries its own fee on top of the load price. Unplug it and empty it a day ahead.
  • Boxes of paper. Heavy, and often the thing people most want to go through. Set those aside for a separate evening.
  • Carpet remnants, paint, chemicals. The carpet can go. The paint and chemicals cannot — see what we can and cannot take and handle those through household hazardous waste.

Check for water before you move anything. A basement that has had moisture — a soft smell, a tide line on cardboard, a box that gives when you lift it — may hold mildew or mold. Open it up, let it air, and if the drywall or carpet itself is wet, that is a remediation question before it is a hauling one.

Stairs and the price

Our pricing is by volume: a basement clear-out typically lands somewhere between a half load ($500) and a full load ($695), with a modest one coming in at a quarter. Stairs are one of the things that can add to that, because they genuinely add time and effort. Tell us up front that it is a basement job, how many flights, and whether the door at the top is a standard width. You will get a range that already accounts for it, and the crew confirms the price on site before loading.

Finishing it

Once the bulk is gone, sweep, run a dehumidifier for a few days, and put the few things you kept on shelves off the floor. A basement stays clear when there is nowhere for a pile to start.
