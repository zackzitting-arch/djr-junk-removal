Renovation Debris: When to Call a Hauler and When to Rent a Dumpster

Published November 6, 2026 · DJR Junk Removal · Meridian, Boise & the Treasure Valley

A kitchen remodel produces a pile. The question is whether you want a container in the driveway for three weeks or a truck for an afternoon. Both are reasonable; they suit different projects.

The one question that decides it

Is the debris already there, or still being generated?

If the demo is done and the old cabinets, countertop, flooring and drywall are sitting in a heap in the garage, a haul is almost always the faster and cheaper option — one visit, we load it, it is gone.

If you are a week into a renovation and generating debris every day, a dumpster earns its keep, because you have somewhere to put each day's mess as you make it. Just remember you are loading it yourself, it occupies the driveway the whole time, and a container on the street may need a permit from the city.

What we take from a remodel

  • Cabinets, countertops, vanities, doors and trim
  • Drywall and plaster
  • Flooring — carpet, pad, laminate, tile that has already been pulled
  • Lumber, framing offcuts, old decking
  • Appliances and fixtures (sinks, toilets, light fixtures)
  • Windows and the old sliding door

Heavy material is priced differently from household junk. Concrete, brick, tile, dirt, rock and gravel weigh many times what furniture does for the same space, so they are not priced purely by volume. We still take them — just tell us up front that it is in the pile, and the range you get will reflect it.

What we cannot take

Paint, solvents, adhesives, fuel and anything containing asbestos. Older homes in the valley may have asbestos in floor tile, mastic, duct wrap or textured ceilings; if your house was built before the 1980s and you are unsure, have the material tested before demolition, not after. Those materials go through regulated channels, and no hauler should tell you otherwise. See the full list.

What it costs

Household construction debris runs on the same volume tiers, $125 minimum up to $695 for a full truck. A bathroom gut is commonly a quarter to half load; a full kitchen with cabinets, counters and flooring is typically a half to full load. Heavy material, stairs and difficult access can add to that, which is why a photo of the pile gets you a better number than a description does. The price is confirmed on site before we load.
