Estate Cleanouts: A Practical Guide for Families

Published August 21, 2026 · DJR Junk Removal · Meridian, Boise & the Treasure Valley

Clearing a parent's house is the hardest cleanout there is, and almost none of the difficulty is physical. What follows is only the logistics half. The rest is not something a checklist helps with.

Before anything leaves the house

Three things, in this order, and none of them are optional.

  1. Find the paperwork. Wills, deeds, titles, insurance policies, tax records, safe deposit keys, military discharge papers. Check filing cabinets, desks, freezers, closet shelves and the backs of drawers. Do this before a single box moves.
  2. Sort out who decides. If there is an executor, they decide. If there is not, get the family aligned on that question first, because every disagreement later is really this disagreement arriving late.
  3. Let people take what matters to them. Give siblings and grandchildren a window and a way to claim things. Doing this before the sorting starts prevents the conversation that begins with "wait, where is grandma's..."

Then work room by room

Not by category. Rooms have edges and finish; categories do not, and "all the photographs in the house" is a task that never ends.

Start with the least emotionally loaded room — usually the garage, the basement or a spare room — and end with the bedroom. By the time you get to the hard rooms you will have a rhythm, and you will have already had the conversations about how you are making these decisions.

Check for value before you assume there is none. Costume jewelry, old tools, cast iron, vinyl, sewing machines, military items and mid-century furniture are all things families routinely discard that a buyer would want. If a room feels like it might hold something, it is worth an appraiser's opinion before it goes in a truck. We would rather tell you to slow down than haul something you regret.

What to do with the rest

After the keeping and the selling, most homes still have the majority of their contents left. That is normal and it is not a failure of the process. It is the part we handle.

An average estate cleanout runs a full load ($495) or more depending on the house, and often takes more than one trip. We do all the lifting and carrying, and we sweep up after.

Give yourself a real timeline

Families routinely plan a weekend for this and then spend four months on it. If the house is going on the market, work backward from the listing date and put actual dates on each room. If there is no deadline, set one anyway — an open-ended estate cleanout has a way of becoming a house nobody visits and nobody sells.
